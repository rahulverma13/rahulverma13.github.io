I'm an Electrical Engineering and Computer Sciences student at UC Berkeley with a minor in Mechanical Engineering. I've spent the last four years at the intersection of hardware and software: designing mechanisms in CAD, machining and printing the parts, wiring the electronics, and writing the control code that ties it all together.

The projects here are the ones I'm proudest of because none of them are standard builds. One turns a quirk of omni-wheel kinematics into a continuously variable transmission. One is a four-stage aluminum telescoping arm that went through 48 design iterations and needed a gain-scheduled controller to tame its changing inertia. One packs a swerve module, a coaxial gearbox and a suspension into the same footprint. And one is a benchmarking tool that squeezes more performance out of supercomputer GPUs and CPUs through software.

I founded and led FIRST Tech Challenge Team 22105 (Runtime Terror), a 15-student team across five schools that qualified for the FTC World Championship. Now I'm pushing further into electronics and PCB design.
