## Overview

A **swerve drive** lets every wheel steer independently, so a robot can drive in any direction while facing any way. In FTC, swerve is already one of the hardest drivetrains to build. We added a twist: **each wheel module can also move up and down** on its own suspension, so the robot can drive over rough terrain and obstacles without losing traction.

Every module has one motor to spin the wheel and one servo to steer it. The challenge is fitting a coaxial gearbox, a steering bearing, and a suspension that still passes drive power into **the same small footprint**, and then making four of them run smoothly together.

## Power transmission

The heart of this design is how power reaches the wheel. Both the motor and the servo are **mounted to the chassis and never move**, yet the module under them can spin endlessly and slide up and down. That's only possible because everything is **coaxial**, sharing the module's vertical axis.

## Module design

<Grid cols={2} align="center">
<Figure src="suspension-swerve/pod-cad.png" variant="plate" ratio="3/4" alt="CAD render of a single module: large ring gear on top, four linear rails with springs around a central shaft, and the wheel below" caption="One module: ring gear, rails, springs and wheel" />
<div>

### Suspension

- **Four MGN7 linear rails** constrain the wheel so it can only move vertically, with no rocking
- **Three compression springs** carry the robot's weight and soak up bumps
- A **sliding bevel gear**, slip-fit on its shaft, keeps the wheel powered through **2.5″ of travel**

### Steering

- The module turns inside **one large bearing**, driven by the servo through a belted pulley and gear
- Because the motor and servo are fixed to the frame, the wheel can **rotate continuously** with no wires twisting

## Electronics & software

- **MT6701 analog absolute encoder** on every module to measure the wheel's steering angle directly
- **Two servo power injectors** to raise the voltage supplied to the steering servos
- Programmed in **Java**, using **second-order swerve kinematics** to reduce drivetrain skew when the robot drives and turns at the same time

## Build process

We 3D-printed prototypes to test the module design before finalizing the CAD, then assembled the modules with aluminum plates. After the first build came months of tuning: **gear spacing** to cut friction, **control software** to reduce driver lag, and getting all of the components to work together smoothly.
